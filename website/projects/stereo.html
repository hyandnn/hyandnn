01 / Stereo perception

Turning sparse observations into usable obstacle geometry.

A cable may be recognizable in an image but poorly represented in depth. I worked on turning that complementary evidence into useful obstacle geometry.

MY ROLE

Thin-obstacle representation, ground estimation, floor-surface post-processing and runtime optimization.

CONTEXT

Stereo-led multi-sensor perception in consumer robotics.

01 / The problem

The label survives. The geometry is sparse.

Thin obstacles can have too few valid depth observations to form a useful spatial representation. Attaching a semantic label to those few points does not address the missing geometric support.

My contribution was a fusion-informed approach to constructing the representation, rather than only assigning semantics to existing points.

02 / Method

Complementary evidence, with different responsibilities.

  1. 01

    Geometric evidence

    Valid depth & surface structure

  2. 02

    Semantic evidence

    Candidate region & interpretation

  3. 03

    Obstacle representation

    Observed support + inferred geometry

Geometric observations supply spatial evidence where depth is valid. Semantic observations supply a candidate region and object interpretation. Their roles are complementary: a semantic detection is not itself a depth measurement.

Combining those cues can support an obstacle representation when observed points alone are sparse. In the demo, light points are observations and the amber dashed segment is an inferred thin-obstacle representation. The distinction remains visible.

03 / Engineering choices

Reuse structure; respect uncertainty.

More useful geometry, not more claimed measurements

Constructing a representation can address sparse observations, but inferred geometry should not be described as additional measured depth.

Different modules, different evidence

I introduced disparity-based ground estimation and developed floor-surface post-processing from complementary cues. I also adapted an existing classification pipeline to stereo and optimized its runtime.

04 / Result & boundaries

Addressing a representation limitation.

The work addressed the sparse-point limitation of thin-obstacle perception. Stereo served as the leading observation source within a multi-sensor perception system.

Depth gaps and uncertain semantic observations remain relevant limitations. The demo makes the observed-versus-inferred distinction inspectable; it does not establish a detection rate or latency.
