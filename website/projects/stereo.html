01 / Stereo perception

Turning sparse observations into usable obstacle geometry.

Thin obstacles can be visible in an image and still be poorly represented in depth. My work focused on making that evidence useful to a robot, alongside ground and floor-surface post-processing.

MY ROLE

Thin-obstacle representation, ground estimation, floor-surface fusion and runtime optimization.

CONTEXT

Stereo-led consumer-robot perception, supported by IMU and odometry information.

01 / The problem

Seeing an object is not enough.

A thin cable may occupy only a small part of the image. Its valid depth observations can be too sparse to form a useful obstacle representation. Simply carrying a semantic label on the available points leaves the geometric gap unresolved.

The engineering question was how to turn complementary evidence into geometry that downstream perception could use, while keeping uncertainty visible.

02 / My contribution

Build the representation around the evidence.

I contributed a fusion-informed way of constructing a thin-obstacle representation, combining geometric observations with semantic evidence. This addressed the sparsity of the available points rather than relying only on labeling them.

Representative outcome

Addressed the sparse-point limitation in thin-obstacle perception by changing how useful obstacle geometry is formed.

That is the central contribution shown here. The interactive scene illustrates the difference between sparse observations and an inferred geometric representation; it is an independent explanation of the problem.

03 / Engineering judgment

Different scene elements need different treatment.

Ground as a geometric reference

I introduced ground estimation from disparity. Establishing the supporting surface provides context for interpreting other scene geometry.

Floor surfaces from complementary cues

I developed floor-surface post-processing that combines geometric and semantic information when model output alone has limited confidence.

Reuse with adaptation

I adapted an existing geometric classification pipeline from another sensor platform to stereo inputs, then optimized its runtime.

Sensor context matters

Stereo was the leading observation source. Supporting IMU and odometry information formed part of the broader perception context.

04 / Scope & limits

A public explanation of the contribution.

The claims above describe my engineering responsibilities and the problem addressed. No latency, detection-rate or benchmark claims are made here because there is no public measurement set to support them.

The demonstration does not reproduce a product's thresholds, data flow or full algorithm. Its geometry is illustrative, and the added representation should be read as a hypothesis rather than an additional depth measurement.

All demonstrations use synthetic data. Project descriptions are limited to publicly shareable responsibilities and conceptual explanations.
