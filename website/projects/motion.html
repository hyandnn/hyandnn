04 / Vision-based climbing analysis

A fixed wall reference for a moving camera.

As the camera scans upward, each frame contains a different subset of fixed wall holds. My master’s research aligned those local observations to a common wall reference for movement analysis.

MY ROLE

Geometric frame-to-template matching, coordinate mapping and movement analysis.

CONTEXT

Master’s research · Robotic Systems Engineering · RWTH Aachen University.

01 / The problem

The holds stay still. The observed window changes.

A video frame covers only part of the climbing wall. During an upward camera scan, holds enter and leave the frame, while their positions in the wall template stay fixed.

Image-coordinate motion therefore mixes camera movement with the climber’s movement. The task was to locate each frame’s observed hold pattern within the common template.

02 / Method

Match the hold pattern, then map the observations.

YOLO-based hold detection supplies wall features in each frame. Geometric matching relates their local arrangement to the fixed hold pattern in the wall template, identifying the observed part of the wall.

The frame-to-template mapping places observations into a common reference. Combined with MediaPipe pose estimation, this supports trajectory, velocity and joint-angle analysis across frames.

03 / Engineering choices

Align the reference before interpreting movement.

Changing subsets, not moving wall points

Matching must accommodate different visible subsets. In the animation, local frame points are derived from the current window over fixed template holds; correspondences change as the window moves.

A common reference is not a full 3D reconstruction

Alignment separates camera framing from movement in the wall reference. Monocular depth ambiguity still limits interpretation of motion away from the wall.

04 / Result & boundaries

Movement compared in the same reference.

The alignment established a common wall reference for trajectory and velocity analysis despite camera movement. The original research videos are unavailable, so the scene uses a constructed wall and movement sequence.

The speed plot is computed from that same synthetic trajectory in normalized wall coordinates. Its horizontal axis is synthetic time; it illustrates consistency between views rather than experimental measurement accuracy.
