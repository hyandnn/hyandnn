04 / Vision-based motion analysis

A common spatial reference despite camera movement.

My master’s research connected video observations to a common wall reference, so camera movement would not define the apparent trajectory of the climber.

MY ROLE

Frame-to-template alignment and integration of visual observations for movement analysis.

CONTEXT

Master’s research · Robotic Systems Engineering · RWTH Aachen University.

01 / The problem

The camera moves; the reference should not.

Image coordinates change when a camera moves, even if the observed scene has not changed. Reading a climbing trajectory directly from those coordinates can mix camera movement with the climber's movement.

The central task was to align local video frames to a common wall template and establish a spatial reference for subsequent analysis.

02 / My contribution

Connect each frame to the wall template.

I used geometric features to align local video frames to a wall template and map observations into that reference. This supported trajectory and velocity analysis despite camera movement.

The broader research pipeline combined hold detection using YOLO and human-pose estimation using MediaPipe, with movement and joint-angle analysis.

Representative outcome

Established a common wall reference for interpreting observations across moving-camera video frames.

03 / Engineering judgment

Alignment is a prerequisite for interpretation.

The middle panel explicitly distinguishes the observed frame from the reference template. Corresponding points communicate the alignment task before the trajectory is interpreted.

Hold detection and pose estimation provide observations; geometric alignment makes observations from different frames comparable. The movement trace illustrates that distinction.

This page uses an independently generated animation because the original research videos are unavailable. The displayed trace is explanatory, not a recovered experiment.

04 / Scope & limits

A monocular reference has limits.

Monocular depth ambiguity remains a limitation. A wall-aligned image reference is not a complete three-dimensional reconstruction of the climber's movement.

The synthetic animation does not establish measurement accuracy or research performance. No unavailable recordings or reconstructed experimental metrics are presented as evidence.

All demonstrations use synthetic data. Project descriptions are limited to publicly shareable responsibilities and conceptual explanations.
